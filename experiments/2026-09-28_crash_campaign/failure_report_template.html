Aqueduct + RedisRaft · RDMA slot migration

AqRaft Failure Campaign

Seven runs of the 30M-key reshard: one without failures and six that kill a node at a critical moment of the migration, including both leaders at once. For each run, what was killed, what the system did, and what clients saw.

runs 2026-09-30 branch aqueduct_broken 30M keys · 3 donors → sg4 · 4,095 slots
Summary

Every failure recovered

In every run all three donor ranges ended up committed on the recipient group, durability was never faked (each INDX_UPD followed a real follower ack) and no process crashed. Client impact ranged from nothing (S3, S5) to 19–23 seconds of reduced throughput when a recipient leader died (S1, S6). Recovery after a donor leader dies takes 16–18 s; after the recipient leader dies alone (S1) it takes 72 s, mostly waiting for the playbook to re-drive.

S1 takes 72 s because two of its ranges wait for the playbook to re-drive them (see open issues). S5 needs no re-send; the one range still in flight commits 3 s after the kill.

After the recovery fixes · 2026-09-30

S1 recovers in 20.6 s instead of 72 s

A second build fixed what made recipient-leader recovery slow, plus two throughput problems. S1, S4 and S6 were re-run on it; all three recovered every range with no faked INDX_UPD and no crashes. HEALTHY, S3 and S4 were also re-run after the follower-pool change alone.

The changes

  • Follower pools registered at startup. Pools are keyed by the device's shared protection domain, not by connection, so a new upstream reuses them. No follower main-thread stall during chain setup (was 0.4–0.6 s per session).
  • Writes go to the recipient leader. The donor redirects writes for migrating slots to the leader it ships to (the re-home target after a failover) instead of cycling through sg4's nodes.
  • Promotion merge once per promotion. Still blocking, so the new leader serves nothing before its held data is merged, but no longer repeated for every in-flight session (16.5 s → 4.1 s).
  • Re-homed donors report to the orchestrator, and a donor whose TRANSFER fails because the recipient died re-homes instead of failing. RDMA writes to a dead peer fail in ~1 s instead of 7.4 s.
  • Durable from the log. Every replica marks slots durable when it applies INDX_UPD, so a promoted follower reports ranges the old leader committed as durable (sg1 is no longer re-sent).
  • Held-copy adoption. A new leader that holds a whole range, with a survivor that holds it too, logs INDX_UPD itself instead of asking the donor to re-send. Not exercised in these runs: the kill came before sg2's batch was forwarded.
  • Playbook leaves its poll loop when a donor reports FAILED or the round's target is no longer the sg4 leader.
First 70 seconds

Client throughput around the migration

All seven runs on one time axis, cut at 70 s. The dip at about 40 s appears in every run, HEALTHY included: it is the donors registering memory before their transfer, while holding a lock client writes need. S1 and S6 drop to zero for about 16 s after the recipient leader dies. That is the promoted sg4 leader's main thread re-merging held slots one at a time for each in-flight session, during which sg4 serves nothing and client threads pile up on its keys.

Setup

How the runs were made

Cluster

  • Donors sg1, sg2, sg3 (leaders redis0, redis1, redis2), each 3-way replicated.
  • Recipient sg4: leader redis3, chain redis4 → redis5.
  • YCSB workload A (50/50 read/update), 2 clients × 100 threads, 10 minutes; the reshard starts after 60 s.
  • Each donor moves 1,365 slots (~2.5M keys) in 4 chunks.

Configuration

  • Dataset restored from the ws30m snapshot (~2 min), leadership moved back to the usual nodes.
  • Donor source pools and 3 recipient landing pools registered at startup.
  • INDX_UPD logged after a follower ack and the leader's merge.
  • Follower acks carried on AppendEntries replies.
  • Kills are kill -9, triggered by a log marker. Every host's logs are copied during the run.

Open issues

What these runs did not settle

  • S1 recovery (fixed after the campaign). The 72 s came from a blocking merge repeated per session, a re-homed donor that never reported to the orchestrator, a TRANSFER failure that did not re-home, and a promoted follower that did not know sg1 was committed. With the fixes S1 recovers in 20.6 s; what remains is election (~1.5 s), the blocking merge (4.1 s) and one re-send of each range that never reached a majority.
  • Leader stall after S3 (open). In the S3 re-run the sg4 leader's main thread stalled once for 374 ms, 2.5 minutes after the migration (stack in update_slot_stats). Cause unknown.
  • Redis watchdog breaks RDMA accept threads. Its alarm signal interrupted rdma_get_request and the accept loops exited. Use it for diagnosis only.
  • Stale AppendEntries ack (fixed during this campaign). Chain session ids restart at 0 on each new recipient leader. In an earlier repeat of S1, a follower's leftover report for the old leader's session 0 reached redis4 17 ms after it set up its own session 0, was taken as that session's ack, and the real ack was then discarded as a duplicate. The batch never committed, the donor re-shipped it and redis4 segfaulted finalizing it twice. The leader now refuses a CHAIN-ACK until it has finished forwarding at least one batch of the session (the report is retried 200 ms later), and a batch can no longer be queued for commit twice. The S1 run shown here used the fixed build.
  • Intermittent RedisRaft crash. In the first run of this campaign, S1's surviving replica redis4 aborted in raft_entry_release (a Raft log entry released twice), taking sg4 below a majority for the rest of the run. It did not recur in S6 or in either repeat of S1. Cause unknown.
  • YCSB client crash. In the first attempt at S2 one YCSB client stopped with a ClassCastException (an array reply where it expected bytes) about 20 s after the donor leader died. This is a client-side robustness bug; the other client and the cluster were unaffected.
  • S4 client stall (fixed after the campaign). Two causes: follower chain setup registered 2.86 GB on the main thread, and clients sent writes to the follower that died and waited on it. Both are fixed; S4 no longer drops to zero.
  • Merges run one session at a time. With the connect delays gone, the leader's serial merges set the length of the migration.
  • Follower key gap. About 3.2% of keys are missing on both followers after a migration, the same set every time; a promoted follower is therefore re-sent whole ranges rather than trusted.
