S1 Orchestrator Stall

After the recipient leader dies, sg2's range is durable on sg4 26.5 s later, but the migration only finishes at 72.1 s. For 39 of those seconds nothing moves: the orchestrator waits for a donor report that never comes, and the playbook waits for the orchestrator until its poll budget runs out.

Topology

Who runs where

Three donor groups share hosts redis0–2; each group is 3-way replicated and has its leader on a different host. The recipient group sg4 lives on redis3–5 and replicates migrated data down a chain. The orchestrator for a round is the sg1 leader, and the playbook's poll loop runs against it.

donor group leader donor replica recipient leader recipient follower
Mechanism

How a round is tracked

  1. The playbook sends RDMA MIGRATE-ALL to the sg1 leader (redis0:8000). That node becomes the orchestrator for the round (orch_id=1) and expects reports from 3 donors.
  2. It dispatches the donors one after another. Each donor's migration worker starts with the orchestrator's address and orch_id.
  3. When a worker ends, it dials back with RDMA MIGRATE-COMPLETE <orch_id> <donor> DONE|FAILED. The orchestrator counts these reports, and the round ends only when all 3 donors have reported.
  4. The playbook polls MIGRATE-ALL-STATUS in a loop and leaves it early only on DONE or FAILED, or when the orchestrator stops answering (the S2 case). Only after the loop does it ask sg4 what is durable and re-send what is missing.
What happened

The stall, lane by lane

The red band marks 26.5–65.3 s: every range is either durable or failed, but nothing reports it, so nothing moves.

Why it hangs

Two of three donors report, and the third never can

sg1 · reported DONE

sg1 committed at −0.52 s, before the kill. The orchestrator counts 1 of 3.

sg3 · reported FAILED

sg3 was dispatched to redis3 0.3 s before the kill. Its REGISTER wait hit the 8 s limit and it reported FAILED at 7.7 s. The orchestrator counts 2 of 3. Unlike the transfer path, this path fails instead of waiting for a re-home.

sg2 · never reported

The sg2 worker saw the recipient die at 0.27 s and waited for the new leader's address. At 18.4 s it started a new worker toward redis4 and exited without reporting. The new worker was started with no orchestrator, so when sg2 committed at 26.5 s nobody was told.

Why 68 seconds

The playbook's poll budget

The poll loop has no notion of "the recipient changed". It stops on DONE or FAILED for the whole round, or after a fixed number of polls. Each poll spawns redis-cli and then sleeps 50 ms.

1,200 polls × (≈7 ms redis-cli + 50 ms sleep) ≈ 68 s  ·  dispatched −2.48 s → loop gives up at 65.3 s

At 65.3 s the loop reports TIMEOUT and the re-drive block starts. It asks the new sg4 leader what it holds with MGN-RESUME-STATUS and re-sends sg1 and sg3. sg1 was already committed, but a promoted follower answers from an empty in-memory batch list, so it reports sg1 as missing too.

Fixes

What removes the wait

1 · The re-homed worker takes over the orchestrator link

Start the re-home worker with the original worker's orchestrator address and orch_id, so it reports DONE when it commits. The round then ends as soon as every donor has reported, at 26.5 s here.

−38.8 sends the stall

2 · sg3 re-homes instead of failing

The REGISTER-timeout path should wait for the re-home, as the transfer path already does, and re-send to the new leader on its own. S1 then no longer needs the playbook to re-drive anything.

no re-drivesg3 self-recovers

3 · The playbook leaves the poll loop sooner

Stop polling as soon as any donor reports FAILED, or when the round's target is no longer sg4's leader. This is a backstop for cases the first two fixes do not cover.

backstopbounded by leader change

Related · Recovery merge and the held copy

Run the promotion merge once and off the main thread (removes redis4's 16.5 s block), and adopt the batch the survivors already hold instead of asking the donor to re-send it. With these and fixes 1–2, S1 should recover in roughly 10–15 s, close to S2 and S6.
